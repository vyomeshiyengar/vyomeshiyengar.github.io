Learning

Help your team understand AI well enough to use it calmly, safely, and consistently.

  • Learn which tools fit your work, what they are good at, and where they need review.
  • Build shared language so everyone understands the setup, not just the person who bought the tool.

Formats: Half-day workshops, ongoing weekly coaching, team training sessions

Implementation

Turn one useful workflow into a working system your team can maintain.

  • Map the current process, find the repetitive handoffs, and build around your existing tools.
  • Leave behind documentation, training, and clear ownership so the system does not depend on me.

Formats: Quick win sprints (1 week, 1 high-impact automation), full operations sprints (4 to 6 weeks, multiple automations with integrations)

Advising

Make steady AI decisions without chasing every new tool that appears.

  • Evaluate tools, vendor claims, and new ideas against your real workflows and constraints.
  • Prioritize what to test next so AI adoption stays useful, measured, and people-centered.

Formats: Monthly advisory retainer with check-ins and priority support

Examples of systems I've helped build

What this looks like in practice

Client Intake and Qualification

Respond to every lead in minutes, not hours

AI sorts new inquiries, checks fit against your criteria, drafts replies, and routes qualified leads. Your team approves the message and decides who follows up.

Review Management

Every review gets a response within hours

Reviews from Google, Yelp, and Facebook come into one place. AI drafts responses in your voice, with a human approval step before anything goes live.

Proposals and Quotes

Proposals go out in minutes

Client details and scope become a branded first draft using your best past examples. You review the substance, pricing, and final recommendation.

Operations and Reporting

Reports that build themselves

Updates from CRM, accounting, and project tools become one clear report. Your team checks the summary, changes, and next steps before sharing.

Scheduling and Follow-Up

No-shows drop from 25% to under 5%

Reminders, missed-appointment follow-ups, and review requests go out automatically. You set the timing, tone, exceptions, and escalation rules.

Content and Communications

First drafts in seconds, not minutes

Approved details can turn into emails, client updates, and social posts. Your team edits for judgment and tone instead of starting from a blank page.
